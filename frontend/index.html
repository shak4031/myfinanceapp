GETTING USEFUL RESULTS

Start with complete, well-organized data

Import statements regularly

Missing statements make totals look lower than they were. At least 3 complete months help the Overview averages; longer histories make trend signals steadier. Compass comparisons use completed months and need at least 3 full months in standard spending categories.

Check categories and transfers

Correct categories make category comparisons meaningful. Transfers between your own accounts are excluded from spending to avoid counting the same money twice. Review unusual classifications before acting on a signal.

Set your household profile

In Spending Compass, enter household size, region and rent/own status for a closer comparison. Until you do, it uses the average U.S. household. Comparisons are guideposts, not targets every household should meet.

Forecasts assume recent patterns continue. They are estimates, can change when new transactions arrive, and are not financial advice.

MFA & SIGN-IN SECURITY

Two-step verification, explained simply

No security background needed. Follow the tutorial or use these guides to set up your phone and avoid losing access.

What are MFA and an authenticator?

Multi-factor authentication (MFA), also called two-step verification or 2FA, adds a phone-generated code to your password. An authenticator is an app that generates short, changing codes after you connect it to MyFinance. These are not SMS texts, email codes, or bank-login codes. Generating a code does not require a mobile signal or internet connection. MFA helps if someone steals your password, but codes can still be phished: enter them only on your genuine MyFinance site and never share them with support.

Watch: set up MFA step by step

Which authenticator app can I use?

MyFinance uses standard time-based TOTP codes with SHA-1, six digits, and a 30-second period for broad compatibility. You can use Google Authenticator or Microsoft Authenticator on iPhone or Android. In Microsoft Authenticator choose Other account, not work/school or Microsoft account. Ente Auth and Aegis are alternatives using the same TOTP settings. Download from official app stores or provider sites. These are standards-compatible choices, not an end-to-end tested or certified app list. An authenticator's own account or backup password is separate from your MyFinance password.

Enable MFA on your phone

  1. Sign in to MyFinance and open Settings & support.
  2. Under Authenticator verification, enter your current MyFinance password and choose Set up authenticator.
  3. Open your authenticator on your phone, choose Add account and Scan QR code, then scan the image displayed by MyFinance on your computer or another screen. In Microsoft Authenticator choose Other account first. The QR includes your account, issuer, SHA-1 algorithm, six digits and 30-second period automatically.
  4. If you cannot scan, expand Cannot scan? Set up manually. Enter the setup key as Secret or Key, your MyFinance email as the account name, and MyFinance as the issuer. Select time-based TOTP, SHA-1, six digits, and 30 seconds (standard defaults). In Google Authenticator choose Enter a setup key and Time based. Other apps may label these options differently; if manual entry is unavailable, scan from another screen.
  5. Back in MyFinance, enter your password again and the current six-digit code, then choose Confirm authenticator within ten minutes.
  6. Save the ten recovery codes somewhere secure outside this browser, then choose I saved the codes; hide them.

On one phone, use the manual option and switch between the browser and authenticator. No SMS or automatic app-launch flow is provided. The QR and setup key contain the same private secret: never share them, send them to support, or keep an unprotected screenshot. MyFinance generates the QR on its own server without an external QR service. MFA is not enabled until confirmation succeeds; confirmation signs out other sessions.

Sign in with MFA and trust a personal browser

Enter your email and password normally. When prompted, open your authenticator, find MyFinance, and enter its current six-digit code. You may select Trust this browser for 90 days on a private device; it is unchecked by default. Returning to that browser still requires your password but skips the code until the fixed expiry or revocation. Ordinary sign-out keeps trust; clearing cookies or using another browser requires a code. Trust recognizes a browser profile, not the physical phone or laptop, and does not extend session timeouts. Never trust a shared or public computer. A stolen password plus trust cookie can bypass the code prompt.

Save and replace recovery codes

A recovery code is a single-use backup for when you cannot use your authenticator. Enter your password and one unused recovery code in place of the phone code at sign-in. That code is consumed; the other unused codes still work. Keep the list securely outside the browser, such as in a protected password manager or a safe printed copy, accessible without signing in to MyFinance. It is different from the authenticator app's own backup or recovery key.

If the set may be exposed or is running low, enter your password and an authenticator code or another unused recovery code in Settings, then choose Replace recovery codes. Every old code becomes invalid, ten new codes are shown once, and trusted browsers are forgotten. Save the new set before leaving. Replacement is not required after every use and does not restore a lost authenticator.

Watch: recovery codes

Lost your phone or moving to a new one?

If you have a secure authenticator backup, restore or transfer the MyFinance entry following that app's instructions and verify the new phone before erasing the old one. Otherwise, sign in with your password and an unused recovery code. In Settings, use your password and a different unused recovery code to disable MFA, then immediately set it up on the replacement phone and save its new recovery codes. Disabling temporarily returns to password-only sign-in and revokes other sessions and browser trust. Replacing recovery codes alone will not connect the new phone.

If a device was lost or stolen, choose Forget all trusted browsers in Settings and change your password if it may be exposed. Forgetting trust signs out other sessions and keeps your current session open. Password reset does not remove MFA. If you lose both your authenticator and recovery codes, contact support for administrator-assisted recovery. An administrator must independently verify your identity before disabling MFA; do not send passwords, setup keys or codes to support. Reset revokes all your sessions, support access, trusted browsers and old recovery codes and attempts a security email. Then sign in with your existing email and password and immediately re-enable MFA on the new phone. There is no automatic email-only reset or guaranteed recovery without identity verification. Keep a separate secure backup of your new recovery codes.

Watch: replace a phone

Why is my code rejected?

Use the MyFinance entry, not a code for your bank or another service. Check SHA-1, six digits, and 30 seconds in the authenticator, and enable automatic date/time on the phone. Enter a fresh code before it changes. A successfully used time step cannot be reused, including the enrollment code: wait for the next code before testing sign-in or changing settings. Recovery codes work only once. If setup expired after ten minutes, start enrollment again and replace the unfinished authenticator entry. If attempts are rate-limited, wait for the indicated period; do not keep retrying. If verification is temporarily unavailable, contact support without sending passwords, setup keys, or codes.

Optional MFA, browser revocation, and limits

Each household member enables MFA for their own login. Demo and support sessions cannot change MFA. Settings lists remembered browsers by timestamps, identifies the current browser when its cookie matches, and lets you forget all trust; up to twenty browsers can be remembered per login. Password changes/resets, MFA confirmation or disabling, recovery-code replacement, and administrator session revocation invalidate trust. To disable MFA, confirm your password and a valid authenticator or unused recovery code; future sign-ins then require only your password. TOTP is not phishing-resistant and cannot guarantee protection from malware or stolen sessions.

TRANSACTIONS

Find the right transaction, from the right account

Use Transactions for exact records and corrections; filter combinations narrow the same list together.

Search and filter your history

Search a merchant, original description, renamed payee, account label, category, or amount. Combine the account selector with date range, category, payment method, and fixed-only filters. Totals and results follow those filters. The account list is built from your records, not a hardcoded bank or card name.

Watch: filter by account

See where a transaction came from

Rows identify the originating account when available, including account type, institution, name, and masked last four. Plaid account details take precedence for linked transactions; statement account details fill in when available. Unassigned means the source could not be determined, not that it came from a particular account.

Rename without losing the original

A friendly name helps make your history recognizable while the original description remains available for context and search. Check both when a bank description is abbreviated or a payment service hides a recipient.

Add a note to one transaction

Open Transactions and choose Add note beneath a description. Write up to 500 characters and select Save note. The note appears below that transaction, is searchable, and travels with your transaction CSV export. Use Edit note to change it, or save an empty note to remove it. Unlike renaming a payee, this does not change similar or future transactions. Notes are visible to your household; do not include passwords or full account numbers. Deleting a transaction also deletes its note.

IMPORTS & DUPLICATES

Which duplicate-review panel should I use?

Both panels are on Transactions. One reviews warnings from imports; the other searches records already saved. Suggestions are not proof of a duplicate, and nothing is merged automatically.

Understand import results

Current CSV, OFX/QFX, PDF, and Plaid imports show imported rows, duplicates, warnings, and errors in the result. Valid new transactions save to history after any required account mapping review. Check the selected account and result before retrying an import.

Clean up possible duplicates in bulk

Open duplicate review, compare each pair, and select only records that are the same real transaction. “Select all unambiguous” excludes ambiguous matches and selects at most 500; competing matches must be resolved individually. Review the batch before merging: the existing record and its settings are kept, extra Plaid rows are removed, and merges cannot be undone.

Watch: bulk duplicate cleanup

Do not merge lookalikes

Similar amounts or descriptions can still be separate purchases, split deposits, or activity from different accounts. In saved-history duplicate review, choose Keep both — different transactions after verifying a pair is different. Both records and their totals remain unchanged, and that pair will not be suggested again. Leave uncertain pairs undecided. The whole merge batch is rejected if a selected pair changed while you were reviewing it.

Review flagged bank imports

Use this panel after a bank sync reports a possible duplicate. It lists bank imports that have an unresolved warning, plus older held imports. Most flagged bank records are already included in history and totals. Choose Keep both if the records are different, or Merge into selected existing if they are the same payment. For an older record labeled Not yet included, Add to history saves it. Clearing a warning does not start a scan of all your history.

Example: a new bank sync flags a payment that looks like one you imported earlier.

Open: Review flagged bank imports

Find duplicates in saved transactions

Use this panel when you have both a bank connection and uploaded statements or manual entries, or when totals look double-counted but no import warning appeared. It scans saved bank-connected records against saved statement/manual records. It does not search for two statement-only or two bank-only duplicates.

Example: the same purchase appears in a statement upload and in bank-connected history. Compare the account, date, description and amount. Merge only the same real transaction; choose Keep both — different transactions for a verified non-duplicate. That exact pair is remembered. Other possible matches still need review. Keeping both in the flagged-import panel also clears that bank record from this scan.

Open Transactions, then Find duplicates in saved transactions

Plaid sync is not the same as a full re-import

Linked accounts use incremental updates when the institution reports changes; institutions control when fresh activity is available. Review connection status and account details under Accounts. A new connection does not automatically make every previously imported statement duplicate-proof—inspect suggested pairs before merging.

GET HELP

Support tickets and support access are different

A ticket asks for help. An access grant lets support inspect financial records only when the household owner chooses to allow it.

Open a support case

In Settings & support, describe what happened, what you expected, the page name and any error message. Description is required (10 to 5,000 characters); category is optional. Choose Submit support case. You will see an MFA- ticket number, and an email notification is sent to myfinanceapp.support@gmail.com. Never include passwords, verification codes or full account numbers.

Track your latest 20 tickets

Each ticket card shows its number, category, opened date and last update. Tap View description to read your original request. Open means received, In progress means support is working on it, and Resolved means completed. Every status change includes a timestamped message in Updates from support explaining progress or resolution; choose Refresh tickets to see changes. History belongs to your sign-in, not everyone in your household.

If an email notification fails, the ticket is still saved. Do not submit it again; support can retry the email. A ticket does not grant access to financial records.

Let support inspect a problem safely

The separate Let support help with a problem panel is optional. Only the household owner can grant access. Choose view-only or view-and-edit access and a duration; keep your case description in the ticket card. View only is recommended. Access expires automatically, can be revoked at any time, and support activity is logged in that panel. Household members can open tickets without being able to grant support access.

ACCOUNTS & CONNECTIONS

Keep account identity and balances clear

Accounts tell you which bank or card supplied a record; connection status tells you when linked data last refreshed.

Correct account details

Review an account's name, type, institution, and masked digits. Correct account type and ownership so transfers between your own accounts are recognized and are not counted as spending. Do not create a second account just because an existing connection needs refreshing.

Watch: Accounts

Connect or refresh with Plaid

Plaid connections can retrieve eligible bank and credit-card activity and balances. Institution update timing varies; refresh may not return new transactions when the institution has none ready. A data connection cannot initiate payments or transfers. You can also use file imports without linking an account.

Understand freshness and history

Accounts shows Last successful Plaid sync separately from Latest saved activity / statement date. A successful check can return no new transactions and does not change the date of an old recorded balance. Failed checks show their attempt time and connection error without advancing the success time. Plaid dates appear only when the saved account uniquely matches the bank, account type and last four digits; otherwise the card explains that no connection is verified. Pending activity and balances can lag. Check the financial institution before relying on a balance or cash-flow estimate.

Statement accounts vs. Plaid accounts

Records from manual imports and linked accounts can carry different source identifiers. MyFinance shows available account metadata but does not guess that two similarly named sources are the same account. Verify ownership and account details in Accounts.

BILLS & PAYDAYS

Know what is expected—and what still needs attention

The calendar is a planning forecast, not a biller's payment record.

Set up fixed bills and real income

Mark recurring bills as Fixed and actual paychecks as Income. Transfers, refunds, and repayments are not paychecks. Choose Auto or Manual for bills when known; manual items are called out for attention. Correct categories separately to describe what the transaction was for.

Watch: bills and income

Set a monthly bill estimate

Choose Set estimate on a bill in Upcoming Payments and enter its normal monthly total. Matching posted payments count toward that total; the unpaid difference remains in the forecast. At 90% or more of the estimate, the bill is approximately complete and the difference is shown but excluded from the cash forecast. This is not confirmation from the biller. Estimates apply from the current month onward and do not automatically change after an unusual payment. The existing estimated date does not schedule multiple installments or follow paychecks.

Why last month's unpaid estimate remains

A missed estimated payment from the immediately previous month stays visible as unpaid. Older months are not carried forward. The estimate clears when a matching payment from that same calendar month is recorded, or when you resolve that occurrence yourself.

Watch: resolve an unpaid bill

Mark paid or ignore one occurrence

“Mark paid” records your confirmation for that bill instance; it does not create a transaction, change a balance, or mark other months paid. “Ignore this payment” dismisses only that month's estimate. A later matching transaction can still be recorded normally.

A bank payment is visible, but the bill still shows due

Check for Pending bank transaction on Transactions. A pending payment can appear in history and totals before the bank posts it; it does not automatically settle a fixed-payment forecast. Sync again after your bank posts it. A uniquely matched posted payment counts toward its own calendar month's bill. Bills without a user-set estimate are settled by one matching payment regardless of its amount. Account identity, payee and competing matches are checked; different accounts and ambiguous payments are not guessed. Use Mark paid only if you independently confirm payment, not just because a pending entry exists.

Stop future projections

Unmarking a recurring bill stops future projections, but does not erase a still-unpaid estimate from last month. Update the source transaction if a bill ended; resolve a carried item separately if it was paid or should not be tracked.

A forecast cannot confirm a creditor's records. Verify actual payment status with the biller or your bank; estimates can be wrong when transaction descriptions, dates, or account history are incomplete.

Search, select several categories, and compare the combined spending you care about.

Find and select multiple categories

Type in the category search to narrow the available choices, then check as many categories as you want. Selected categories appear as removable chips, and totals and charts reflect the selected group. Clear the selection to return to the unfiltered category view.

Watch: categories and smart rules

Fix a category or future merchant rule

Correct an individual transaction when it is a one-off. If future imports from that merchant should use the same category, create or update a merchant rule in Manage categories. Review similar-transaction prompts before applying changes across your history.

Transfers are not spending

Assign both sides of transfers between your accounts correctly. Otherwise a move between accounts can look like income or an expense and distort category totals, trends, and forecasts.

DEBT & SAVINGS GOALS

Plan with your numbers, not autopilot

Planning tools model scenarios; they do not move money or guarantee a finish date.

Build a debt payoff plan

Add each balance, interest rate, and minimum payment. Compare avalanche, snowball, and available Pro strategies at the same monthly budget. Review the cash-flow assessment and keep a cushion before increasing payments. Updating the plan changes projections—not lender payments.

Watch: your debt-free plan

Choose and track savings goals

Set a target, date, and amount already saved. Choose how goals share the modeled surplus. Record an actual contribution after saving; that action updates progress but does not transfer money. Bank transfers are not automatically treated as goal contributions.

Watch: savings goals

What each savings goal field means

Target amount is the total you want saved. Current savings is money already set aside for this goal, not the cost of purchases. Target date is the funding deadline, not a merchant payment date. Priority ranks goals (1 is highest); the selected funding strategy determines how that rank is used. Icon and notes describe the goal. Include in my monthly plan enables funding; pausing keeps the goal without allocating money.

Monthly amount is a savings request, not a bill

Leave Monthly amount blank for automatic funding from the target date and goals budget. Entering a number requests that amount every month while the goal is included and not fully funded. Fixed requests get funding before automatic goals, but are capped by the remaining goal balance and the shared budget; they are not guaranteed. Zero requests no monthly savings. Check the goal card's This month amount to see the actual allocation.

How goals share the budget

The default goals budget uses modeled surplus after bills, reserved debt minimums, and debt extras. You can set a shared monthly goals budget, but increasing it does not create cash. Fixed monthly requests are funded first. Priority order funds dated automatic goals in rank order; Nearest deadline puts the earliest deadline first; Balanced shares automatic funding in proportion to need. Undated goals receive leftover funds. Review the budget, allocations, and warnings before committing money.

Why is my goal missing from upcoming payments?

Set aside on day chooses the monthly calendar day (1–31), not a purchase date. A blank day uses the first projected payday, or day 1 if there is none; days beyond month-end use the last day. The calendar shows included, unfinished goals with a positive allocation, or an existing adjustment for that month. A day already passed is treated as past and does not reduce future runway. Check Pro access, inclusion, current savings, This month, the shared budget, and the set-aside day, then reopen the Dashboard. If the allocation is positive and the day is upcoming but the entry is still absent, report it through Support rather than entering a duplicate.

Adjust this month without changing the long-term plan

Select an upcoming flexible goal entry on the Dashboard to change its amount for this month, skip with $0, or return to the planned amount. Next month returns to the long-term plan. This edits a savings reservation, not an actual transfer or a merchant payment. Future calendar allocations are estimates based on the current plan, not confirmed future payments.

What does Plan type mean?

Plan type is a goal preset, not an expense schedule or funding strategy. General savings fits renovations, trips, and other goals. Car · six-month plan changes the default icon and fills a date six months ahead only when the date is blank; an existing date stays unchanged. College changes the label and default icon. Emergency fund also enables emergency-fund-specific guidance. These presets do not create purchases, special accounts, or automatic bank transfers.

Example: a renovation purchase versus saving for it

A $5,500 target, $0 saved, a November 20 deadline, $3,500 monthly amount, and day 9 means: request up to $3,500 of savings on the 9th each month toward a $5,500 savings target. It does not schedule a one-time Home Depot purchase on November 9. The actual allocation may be smaller if the goals budget is insufficient. Use Plan purchase on the goal for the merchant, one-time amount, purchase date, and payment method instead. Do not record a purchase as savings or mark it as a recurring fixed bill just to force it onto the calendar. Saving and buying are separate actions; check the Dashboard so you do not reserve the same cash twice.

Plan a one-time goal purchase

  1. Open a goal and choose Plan purchase.
  2. Enter the merchant or description, amount, and purchase date.
  3. Choose cash/debit for cash out on the purchase date, or credit card and enter the explicit card-payment date. For a card, plan only additional cash not already included in another card-payment estimate.
  4. Save and check the Dashboard's next-30-day cash runway. The one-time purchase is included even when the savings budget is $0 or goal funding is paused; it does not repeat monthly. Past unresolved purchases remain for review.
  5. Edit dates or amounts as plans change. Cancel a purchase you will not make. Mark recorded only when the cash payment is reflected in imported/current balances, or included in an existing card-payment forecast, so it is not counted twice. Return to planned only if it is still an additional future outflow.

Purchase status never changes Current savings or creates a transaction. There is no automatic matching to imported transactions. Clicking a purchase on the Dashboard opens Goals for management. Deleting a goal also deletes its purchase plans.

Understand funding readiness and deadline pace

The funding ledger shows recorded income, modeled expenses, debt extras, available cash, the shared savings budget, and allocations. A negative amount before goals explains why automatic funding is $0; it does not prove you have no existing savings. Each goal separates its monthly request, actual allocation this month, next scheduled deposit, and amount needed per deposit by the exact deadline. The model counts upcoming monthly deposit dates, clamps days beyond month-end, and excludes a set-aside day already passed. If no deposit fits before the deadline, change the day or date. Savings progress changes only when you record real money set aside.

You may choose a custom budget above modeled spare cash, but it is explicitly flagged as unsupported and requires existing funds or changed income/spending. Try a monthly scenario to see a finish date and affordability warning without changing settings. Scenarios assume that trial amount goes to this goal alone every month; other goals and real-world changes still need review. Deadline and allocation calculations are estimates, not promises.

Record savings only after you save

Record savings increases Current savings and reduces the amount still needed. It does not make a bank transfer, settle a calendar entry, or record spending against the goal. Imported savings transfers do not automatically update individual goals. Assign only money actually reserved for this goal; do not count the same savings toward multiple goals. The app does not track a goal's purchase ledger or automatically subtract purchases from savings progress.

Debt coaching and spending scenarios

The debt-free plan assesses imported history, minimums, savings allocations, existing extras, and a planning cushion before suggesting acceleration. Missing history or a shortfall can block a suggested increase. Review the spending opportunities and test a monthly extra or one-time payment to compare payoff timing and interest. Scenarios alone do not change saved budgets or send payments; applying a supported change updates the plan only. Check real cash, due dates, and emergency reserves first.

Mortgage planner and payoff simulator

In the debt-free plan, open the Mortgage planner and enter the original loan amount, APR, term, first payment month, optional current balance, and escrow. Review the principal-and-interest schedule, yearly breakdown, and estimated interest. Test extra monthly principal, annual extras, a dated lump sum, or bi-weekly payments. These are simulations, not lender instructions or scheduled bank withdrawals. Verify balances, fees, escrow, and how your lender applies extra principal before acting.

Why a plan can change

New transactions, corrected account balances, missing statements, changed goals, or edited debt details can alter estimated surplus and payoff dates. Treat every result as a scenario based on the information currently in the workspace.

Start, refresh, and understand progress

  2. Open Financial Health and review your typical monthly split and suggested focus areas.
  3. Choose Start my 90 days to save the start date and available spending baseline.
  4. Open the current week's step, read its data-based detail, and use its link to the relevant tool.

Progress is saved in the household workspace. Automatic completion reflects the available records, not independent verification that every real-world action happened. Removing a manual completion mark does not override an automatic Done status. Start over clears the program start, baseline, and manual marks, not your transactions, accounts, debts, or goals; data-based completions may remain.

Read the health score and spending buckets

The guide compares take-home income with fixed costs at or below 30%, discretionary spending at or below 30%, debt payments at or below 25%, and money left to save at or above 15%. Mortgage payments count as debt here, not fixed expenses. Each measure contributes up to 25 points to the 0–100 score. Labels are Strong (85+), Steady (65–84), Stretched (40–64), and Under pressure (below 40). Without usable income history, no score is shown. These are planning guidelines, not universal household budgets; money left over is not proof that it was saved.

Month 1: Know your numbers

  1. Week 1 — Review where it goes: review the fixed, discretionary, and debt split; three completed recorded months enable automatic completion.
  2. Week 2 — Cut the leaks: investigate large discretionary categories. The program checks for at least a 10% reduction in the recent two-month average against the saved baseline.
  3. Week 3 — Automate saving: arrange transfers with your bank yourself. Recorded savings transfers of at least 10% of modeled take-home trigger Done; this does not verify that a bank automation exists.
  4. Week 4 — Tackle card debt: review balances and rates in the Debt-free plan. A saved debt budget at least $50 above included minimums triggers Done; this is a plan setting, not proof of payment.

Month 2: Build the foundation

  1. Week 5 — Build an emergency fund: the program checks tracked savings for a $1,000 starter fund and three months of modeled expenses; it does not verify that those savings are exclusively reserved for emergencies.
  2. Week 6 — Start investing: review investment options appropriate to your needs and risk. A tracked investment account triggers Done; it does not verify holdings, contributions, or investment suitability.
  3. Week 7 — Raise your income: review the income trend and consider practical opportunities; mark completion yourself.
  4. Week 8 — Set a goal with a number: enter a savings target and date. A dated goal triggers Done; this does not mean it is funded or affordable.

Month 3: Lock it in

  1. Week 9 — Use cards on your terms: review card interest and listed balances. Two recorded months without card interest and no included card balance can trigger Done; missing card records can make this incomplete.
  2. Week 10 — Know your net worth: review tracked cash, savings, and investments minus included debts. Known assets or debts trigger Done; untracked property and obligations are absent.
  3. Week 11 — Re-check your spending: compare the recent two-month discretionary average with the starting baseline and mark completion after review.
  4. Week 12 — Look ahead: plan goals within one year, beyond one through five years, and beyond five years (the program's 10-year bucket). Having dated goals in all three buckets triggers Done. Schedule your own quarterly check-in.

Which program or tool should I teach?

Financial Health is the ongoing weekly checklist and saved baseline. Financial Checkup is a separate Pro snapshot of historical income, modeled outflows, dated-goal savings pace, and next steps; it does not start the 90-day program. Debt-free plan models repayment, coaching, and mortgage scenarios. Savings Goals models money set aside and tracks manually recorded progress. Analytics and Spending Compass investigate patterns and spending opportunities. The Dashboard shows upcoming bills, expected income, and goal/debt reservations. Advanced analytics, debt, and savings planning require Pro even when linked from the Basic health program.

Teaching rule: distinguish recorded facts, modeled estimates, and manual completion. The program never opens an account, invests, automates a transfer, sends a payment, or confirms affordability. Correct incomplete records before treating a score or Done badge as evidence.

ANALYTICS · OVERVIEW

Your history, signals and near-term picture

Use this view to notice a change, identify where it came from, then decide what to inspect or adjust.

At-a-glance measures

Six quick checks summarize the current month and recent household finances.

Spent this month

Spending since the first of this month, compared with the same point last month. Useful for spotting a change in pace; partial months should only be compared at the same point in time.

Projected

Estimated month-end spending, including bills still due and typical flexible spending for the remaining days. Treat it as an early warning, not a promise.

Avg monthly

Average monthly spending across the last 3 full months, shown beside average income. A quick baseline for whether the household is generally spending more or less than it brings in.

Savings rate

Average income left after spending, as a share of income over those 3 full months. It cannot be measured without recorded income and may be negative when spending exceeds income.

Fixed vs flexible

The share of average monthly spending marked as fixed bills. It helps distinguish committed costs from more adjustable spending; incorrect fixed-bill labels distort this share.

Daily burn

This month's spending pace per elapsed day, alongside days left in the month. Use it with the projection to see whether the current pace is sustainable, not as a spending target.

Month-end forecast

Plots this month's spending pace beside last month and estimates the finish, splitting the estimate into bills still due and expected flexible spending. Check it after large purchases or as bills come due; compare like-for-like months and remember that new data can move the estimate.

6-month cash flow

Projects the recorded balance forward if typical income and spending continue. The starting point comes from your latest recorded account balance; the line highlights the projected balance, lowest point and typical surplus or shortfall. Update balances and fixed bills, and treat the result as a scenario—not a guaranteed runway.

Monthly spend trend

Shows up to 12 months of spending, a 3-month moving average and income. The average smooths one-off bumps so you can see direction; use the monthly bars to find when a change began. Missing statement months can make the trend misleading.

Recurring charges not marked fixed

Finds steady, scheduled charges that are not already marked as fixed bills, estimates their monthly/yearly cost and flags detected price increases. Review the merchant, amount and cadence: a repeating charge is not automatically a bill or subscription. Mark genuine bills fixed so they can feed the bill calendar and forecast; review unwanted charges with the provider.

Category signals

For each spending category, compare this month, the same point last month, 3- and 6-month averages, share of spending, trend and volatility. “Running hot” means the projected month is at least 15% above its 3-month average; “Cooling” is a direction, not proof that spending is healthy. Sort the columns, then open a category to inspect its transactions.

Unusual activity

Surfaces individual charges or category spending far outside the usual pattern. Use it as a review queue for possible duplicates, miscategorized items or genuine one-offs—not an automatic fraud alert. Confirm against the statement before changing anything.

Top merchants

Ranks where the last 90 days of spending went, including transaction count, average purchase, share and change. It is useful for finding a concentrated or growing expense; a high total alone does not tell you whether the spending is avoidable.

Weekly rhythm

Totals recent spending by weekday and weekend, with an average week and quietest day. It can prompt questions about routines, but it does not adjust for how many of each weekday occur or why purchases happened. Treat small differences as descriptive, not behavioral evidence.

SPENDING COMPASS

Your patterns, with outside context

Compass is not another forecast. It combines your category history with published Consumer Expenditure Survey benchmarks, adjusted for household size, region and housing. Set a time period for each panel; short or incomplete periods can produce unstable comparisons.

Compass plan and headline

Start here. It estimates potential monthly savings for categories where spending is at least $10 above a suggested level and is either above a relevant benchmark or showing an upward trend. Suggested levels are based on your own lower-spending months, typical household spending or—when spending is seasonal—the same months last year. The opportunity is a scenario, not a promised saving or a requirement to spend like another household. Review the category evidence before setting a target.

Where your money flows

Shows how average monthly income is divided among fixed bills, everyday categories, debt minimums and money left over. Use it to see the broad shape of your budget and which category contributes most. It reflects imported and classified activity; it cannot include bills or debt payments that were never recorded.

Where the signals overlap

Combines three separate checks—above a similar household, trending up and unusually high for your own history. Categories with multiple signals deserve earlier review; one signal alone is a prompt to watch, not a verdict. This adds benchmark and unusual-month context beyond Overview's category trend table.

Who gets your money

Totals all non-transfer payments to each merchant in the exact completed months shown, including fixed bills and large purchases. Payments renamed to the same chosen name are combined. Select a bubble for precise money-out totals, separate credits, net outflow and a list of counted transactions. Credits are not automatically treated as refunds or subtracted from spending. The chart shows the largest 36 merchants; its overall spending total includes all merchants. Compare with Transactions using the same months: Transactions' “Last 3 months” includes the current month, while Compass uses completed months. Missing statements can understate any total.

Hot and cold months

A category-by-month view that makes seasonality, recurring peaks and unusually quiet months easier to see. Hover or focus a cell for the exact value. Compare complete months only; a missing statement is not a zero-spending month.

Your normal range

Compares recent category spending with its historical range and shows how exceptional the latest month is relative to prior months. Use it to distinguish a routine fluctuation from a genuine spike, while checking that the history is complete and relevant.

Technical readings

An optional detail table for average vs. typical spending, momentum, moving-average crossovers, volatility bands, how many recent months rose, and whether change came from purchase frequency or purchase size. It is for investigating the “why” behind a signal; the headline and plan are enough for a quick review.

Benchmark values come from the cited U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Consumer Expenditure Survey, not other MyFinance users. Contracted bills such as mortgage, rent, loans, insurance and internet are not compared. Survey averages are context, not a judgment about what you should spend.

WHAT IS DISTINCTIVE?

A candid note on usefulness

Strongest decision support

The month-end forecast, category signals and recurring-charge review each suggest a concrete next step: check the pace, investigate a category, or verify a repeating payment. Compass adds a different lens by combining personal trends with an external household benchmark.

Most useful together

Overview's “Running hot” signal compares a category with your own recent average; Compass's overlap adds whether it is above a similar household and unusually high for you. These are related but not identical. Use Overview to spot movement, Compass to add context, and transaction details to decide.

Worth evaluating

Weekly rhythm currently reports weekday totals and a peak day, but gives little context for day-of-week frequency or a practical action. If it does not change a decision, it may be a candidate to simplify or replace with a normalized comparison. Top merchants can also be descriptive unless the user can drill into a specific opportunity. These are product questions, not conclusions from your personal data.
